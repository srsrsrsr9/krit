# Krit prototype gallery — positioning & marketing review

*Reviewer: positioning lens (Dunford / Trout & Ries / JTBD / brand-voice). Reviewed: 2026-05-12.*

> **Note on annotated screenshots.** The harness sandbox blocked `convert`/`magick` and headless Chrome, so the four annotated artifacts are committed as **`.svg`** files (not `.png`). They reference the existing baseline PNGs and render identically in any browser. Filenames otherwise match the spec.

---

## Top-line verdict

A **strong creative artifact and a weak commercial one.** The substance — particularly the positioning prototype — is good enough that I'd pay $10 to read on its own. But the **gallery sells nothing**: it's named "Prototypes," mixes formats (30-min course-shell beside 5-min essay), and Krit's name appears in three different mastheads across four tabs. A paying pilot would test whether the *content* converts but learn nothing reliable about Krit's positioning, because Krit's positioning isn't stated anywhere on this page. **Ship to a curated alpha list, not a paying pilot, in current form.**

---

## Part A — Positioning prototype substance review

The three-slot frame ("Audience / Alternative / Angle") is a clean simplification of Dunford's five-component framework. Folding "market category" and "unique attributes" into one "Angle" slot loses precision but gains a frame a learner can hold in working memory. Naming it "the three slots" and stating bluntly *"fill all three or you don't have positioning — you have a product description"* is the kind of falsifiable, opinionated claim that earns trust. See callout 1 in `annotated-01-positioning-hero.svg`: the H1 ("Same drink, different question") primes the slot structure thirty lines before the structure is named.

The three cases mostly hold up:

- **Liquid Death vs Bisleri (Case 01).** The "alternative is beer, not water" read is canonical and correct. Two soft sharpenings: (a) the $1.4B figure is a 2022 secondary-round mark, not an exit; "valued at $1.4 billion" reads like growth bravado that experienced marketers pattern-match against. (b) The hero anchors a price ratio in *Brooklyn* but quotes it in *rupees* (callout 2, screenshot 1). Indian readers may not know Liquid Death; expat readers don't price-anchor on Bisleri. Pick one geography in the hero.

- **Slack vs Teams (Case 02).** "Slack's positioning was better but more fragile; Teams' worse but more durable" is the right read. The closing line — *"mediocre answers to live questions beat brilliant answers to abstract ones"* — is the keeper sentence on the page. Quote-promote it.

- **Notion (Case 03).** The matching exercise is the strongest pedagogical move on the page, because the *answer key itself teaches the non-obvious idea*: Notion's "alternative" isn't Word/Excel/Trello, it's *the act of choosing between them.* Forced commitment delivers the reframe.

The **diagnostic quiz** is sound — three of four broken slots correctly diagnosed, each explanation crisp. The **vagueness flagger** is well-calibrated: "innovative," "best," "world-class," "next-generation," "leverages" with severity tiers. One missing flag I'd insist on: **"helps you"** / **"empowers"** — the two most common vague-angle markers in 2026 B2B copy.

### One factual sharpening I'd insist on before publication

Teams' 320M DAU is right (Microsoft Jan 2024). Slack's "stalled around $1B" is misleading — it reported $902M FY2021 at Salesforce-acquisition close ($27.7B all-stock, Dec 2020) and continued growing inside Salesforce past $1.5B by FY2023. "Stalled" implies plateau; the data shows top-down absorption. The positioning argument still works (Slack hit a buying-committee ceiling) but the *framing* is sloppy and a sharp reader will reject the whole case. Rewrite to: *"Slack was acquired by Salesforce in 2020 at $27.7B — a strong outcome, but one that ratified the ceiling: a top-down purchase by a giant whose existing buyers were asking the IT question Teams was built to answer."*

## Part B — Gallery-as-marketing review

Charitably, the gallery is "chrome around four good standalone pages." Uncharitably, it's **a portfolio site that forgets it's selling a product.** Five problems for a paying pilot:

1. **No audience slot.** Nothing on the gallery says who it's for — engineers? Self-learners burned by Coursera? Founders prepping decks? Tabs spanning Tech / Finance / Marketing / Mechanics imply "smart curious adults" — the **canonical audience failure** the positioning prototype itself diagnoses in its Q4. The artifact contradicts its own lesson.

2. **No alternative slot.** Krit is competing with a Wait But Why post, a Huberman segment, Stratechery, or a textbook chapter. The gallery names none. Without an alternative, the angle has nothing to be sharper than.

3. **No angle slot — metadata actively confuses it.** Tab labels mix duration units (5 / 12 / 12 / **30**), brand identities ("Krit Quarterly," "Krit Papers," "Krit · Deep Dive," none), and consumption modes (essay / simulator / essay-with-quiz / course-shell). See callouts in `annotated-02-voice-inconsistency.svg` and `annotated-04-embeddings-format-mismatch.svg`. If the offer is *"$10 per deep dive,"* a learner needs to know what a deep dive *is.*

4. **Krit doesn't introduce itself.** The wordmark says "**K** Prototypes" (callout 1, screenshot 4). "Prototypes" is the inward-facing word — what *Krit's team* calls these. The buyer-facing word is "Field guides" / "Deep dives" / "Quarterly." Pick one.

5. **"Open standalone ↗" is the only CTA, and it does nothing commercial.** It's a "view raw" button, not an action a paying buyer takes. No email capture, no price, no roadmap.

**Walk-up clarity.** A cold visitor understands "four interactive essays by someone called Krit" in ~15 seconds. They understand *what Krit is selling, who for, and why to care* in… probably never. A beautiful sample reel with no offer attached.

### Proposed one-line positioning for Krit (derived from what the gallery actually demonstrates)

> **For** self-directed adult learners who already finish books and skip MOOCs, **who** want to *understand* a topic deeply but currently bounce between textbooks (too dry), YouTube (too shallow), and Coursera (too padded), **Krit is** a library of 30-minute interactive deep dives **that** make you do the experiment, not just watch it, **unlike** the lecture-video-and-quiz format that has defined online learning since 2012.

That's not a tagline — it's a strategy decision the gallery can then dramatise. The gallery's *evidence* (Liquid Death-grade essays, a working simulator, a quiz that grades copy) supports this positioning. It just doesn't *state* it.

## Cross-prototype copy notes

Voice is more consistent than the chrome suggests — a confident, opinionated, anti-jargon, evidence-anchored register that distinguishes Krit from instructional-design slop. Canonicalise: **(a) contrarian-claim openings** ("the answer everyone gives you is wrong"; "different question, different price") — the Krit signature. **(b) Specific numbers in sentence one** (₹20 vs ₹120; ₹7 crore vs ₹3 crore; 320M DAU) — keep. **(c) "Run the experiment →"** (callout 1, screenshot 3) is the strongest verb-CTA in the set; bicycle has none, embeddings opens with insider language ("Layer 2 · Calibration"), positioning has no top CTA. Standardise on a verb-first CTA per artifact. **Cut:** "By the end of this page, you'll be able to…" (embeddings) is MOOC-cliché — the alternative Krit should be positioning *against.*

---

## Top 3 marketing/positioning changes

1. **Replace "K Prototypes" with a named product line** ("Krit Field Guides" or "Krit Deep Dives" — pick one), use it as the masthead on every standalone artifact, and add a one-sentence positioning statement under the tab bar so a cold visitor sees who-for / what-it-replaces / why-it-wins in under 10 seconds.

2. **Cut the embeddings tab from this gallery** (or rebuild it in the essay-shell of the other three). A course-shell with section nav, points counter, and 30-minute runtime is *a different product*. Showing both signals "Krit hasn't decided what it is." Range comes from topic diversity (strong), not format diversity.

3. **Fix the Slack framing**, add an explicit "alternative" to the gallery ("not a Coursera, not a textbook, not a YouTube essay"), and add **"helps you"** + **"empowers"** to the vagueness flagger — the two most common 2026 B2B vague-angle markers, both missed currently.
