### Speech block 2 — *Mental model: just-enough observability*  *(target 1:30 – 3:50)*

[thoughtful] Treat your well-being like a small, well-instrumented system.

[matter-of-fact] You don't need a dashboard with forty metrics. You need three numbers, captured cheaply, looked at weekly. Engineers call this *just-enough observability.* Therapists call it *self-monitoring.* Same idea, different priesthood.

[firm, slowly] Anything past three numbers is procrastination dressed as instrumentation.

[wry] I'll put that more directly. The most common pattern I see in this domain is people building elaborate trackers as a way to avoid actually changing anything. A new dashboard feels like progress. It is not progress. It is decoration.

[curious] Why three numbers? Because three is the smallest number that captures the structure of what's happening, and the largest number you'll actually keep up with for ten weeks. Two numbers misses the structure. Five numbers gets abandoned by week three.

[matter-of-fact] The criteria for a good well-being metric. One — *cheap to capture.* Two minutes max. Two — *empirically grounded.* It needs to come from a real, validated instrument, not a vibe. Three — *covers a distinct dimension.* No two of your three should measure the same thing.

[firm] We'll meet the three numbers in the next chapter. They satisfy all three criteria. Use them. Don't substitute.

### Speech block 3 — *Move 1: the three-number stack*  *(target 3:50 – 7:50)*

[firm] Move one. The three-number stack. Three numbers. Once a week. Same day, same time. Two minutes total.

[matter-of-fact, slowly] Number one. *PANAS-short.* The Positive and Negative Affect Schedule. Ten emotions, rate each from one to five. Five positive — interested, excited, strong, enthusiastic, alert. Five negative — distressed, upset, guilty, scared, jittery. Sum each side separately.

[curious] Now — important moment in the course. Why measure positive AND negative affect, instead of one combined happiness score? [pause] Because it turns out they are not opposites. They are *partly independent dimensions.* You can be high on both — *busy and stimulated but anxious.* You can be low on both — *flat and calm.* A single zero-to-ten happiness score collapses these into the same number, and they describe wildly different lives.

[wry] Picture a four-quadrant grid. Top-right — high positive, high negative — busy and anxious. The corporate-Bharat modal point. Top-left — high positive, low negative — *flow.* The state we're often actually after. Bottom-left — low positive, low negative — flat and calm; common in retired uncles, sometimes desirable. Bottom-right — low positive, high negative — flat and miserable; this is the one that needs intervention.

[emphatic] Different interventions move different quadrants. You need to know which one is moving. Single-score happiness can't tell you.

[firm, slowly] Number two. *The Cantril ladder.* On a zero-to-ten ladder where ten is the best possible life for you, where do you stand today? Single integer. The Gallup World Poll uses this; it's comparable across years and across humans.

[matter-of-fact] Number three. *Average sleep hours, last seven nights.* Boring. Decisive. Sleep is the highest-correlation single behavioral input to next-day affect. Anyone who's pulled an all-nighter knows this in their bones; the data backs it up.

[curious] That's it. Three numbers. PANAS, Cantril, sleep. Sunday at nine p.m., copy a small template into a recurring note, fill it in, close it. Two minutes.

[switching to Hinglish, warm] Software engineer. Salary acchi. Sprint mein deliver ho raha hai. Manager khush. PANAS positive: seventeen. [pause] Lekin Sunday raat ko anxiety: sleep aage ki sprint ka, scared of next quarter, jittery about RSU vesting. PANAS negative: eighteen. [pause]

[returning to English, dry] Single score asks "happy or not happy?" and you stall. Dual axis says: *busy and anxious quadrant. Both numbers high.* That's a precise diagnosis. Now you know which lever to pull.

### Speech block 4 — *Move 2: the fourteen-day intervention loop*  *(target 7:50 – 11:40)*

[firm] Move two. *The fourteen-day intervention loop.*

[matter-of-fact] Tracking is necessary. Tracking alone is not the experiment. The experiment is — pick one intervention from this course, run it for fourteen days, compare your check-in numbers before and after.

[brisk] Good interventions to test, each isolating one variable. *Variety swap* — rotate three different morning routines for fourteen days versus a fixed one. *Weekly gratitude* — three specific entries every Sunday for two Sundays. *One engineered friendship slot* — a recurring thirty-minute call. *Sleep floor* — hard rule, seven-plus hours every night, no exceptions.

[firm, slowly] One intervention. Fourteen days. Three numbers before. Three numbers after. [emphatic] That is the entire scientific method, in a tracksuit.

[curious] Walk through what fourteen days actually looks like.

[matter-of-fact] *Day zero. Baseline.* Take all three measurements. Don't read them too hard yet — single points are noise.

*Day one through six.* Run the intervention. Don't add a second one. Don't tweak. Run.

*Day seven. Mid-check.* Take all three measurements again. Note any drift. [emphatic] Resist the urge to add a second intervention. The interesting data has not arrived yet.

*Day fourteen. Final check.* Take all three measurements. Compare to day zero. Crucially — also compare to day seven. A real effect typically grows from week one to week two; a placebo bump tends to fade.

*Day fifteen. Decide.* Keep, modify, or drop. If kept, install permanently using the four-part rewire from lesson three.

[firm, slowly] One critical rule for reading results. *A real effect shows up in two of the three numbers — not just the one you hoped would move.*

[wry] This rule is doing a lot of work. The most common bug in self-experimentation is cherry-picking the metric that moved and ignoring the others. If your variety swap moved Cantril and PANAS positive but tanked sleep — that's a real but partial effect with a known guardrail to add. Not a clean win. Not a failure. A tradeoff. Most interventions land somewhere in the tradeoff zone.

[firm] If only the favorite metric moves, you have a placebo, or noise. If two of three move, you have signal. If all three move, you have something worth installing permanently.

[matter-of-fact] One more thing. *Inconclusive is a valid outcome.* If two weeks pass and nothing moves more than a point, that means run another fourteen days, or stack a small additional move. It does not mean panic.

[switching to Hinglish, warm] Saturday raat eleven baje. Notion mein twenty-two-widget dashboard banaya — mood emoji column, weekly graph, color-coded sleep bar. Bahut santosh hua. [chuckles] Monday: ek baar dekha. Wednesday: yaar update karna hai dashboard, nahi kiya. Friday: bhool gaya kahaan tha. Mahine baad: link bhi kho gaya.

[returning to English, dry] Three numbers. One note app. Sunday nine p.m. Anything fancier and you are doing well-being theater, not well-being.

### Speech block 5 — *Try it, reflect, and where you are now*  *(target 11:40 – 14:00)*

[warm] Time to design *your* fourteen-day experiment.

[firm] Specify five things. [pause] One. The single intervention. Not three. One. Two. The exact day you'll take baseline. Three. The exact day for the day-seven mid-check. Four. The exact day-fourteen decision. Five. What success looks like, in numeric terms — not a feeling.

[matter-of-fact] Example. *Intervention — weekly gratitude, three specific entries every Sunday for two Sundays. Baseline — this Sunday at nine p.m. Mid-check — next Sunday at nine p.m. Final check — Sunday after that, at nine p.m. Success criterion — Cantril ladder up by at least one point, AND PANAS positive sum up by at least three, AND no worsening of PANAS negative.*

[firm] That is what an actual self-experiment looks like. Numeric. Dated. Falsifiable. Anything looser and you're back to vibes.

[curious] Worth pausing on the success criterion. *Up by at least one point.* You should pre-commit to the threshold *before* you see the data. Otherwise the threshold drifts to wherever your data happened to land — which is exactly the bug we're trying to avoid.

[wry, slowly] Where you are now. [pause]

[warm] You walked in believing happiness was a feeling that happened to you. You are walking out with — one mental model. The thermostat. Four named bugs — miswanting, the arrival fallacy, the intensity arms race, the G.I. Joe Fallacy. Two cheat codes — structured gratitude, engineered connection. And a fourteen-day experimental loop with three measurement points and a single variable. [pause]

[firm] That's a complete system. [emphatic] Ship it.

[brisk] Five takeaways for this lesson. [pause] One. Three numbers — PANAS, Cantril, sleep — are enough; more is procrastination. Two. Positive and negative affect are not opposites; track both. Three. One intervention, fourteen days, before-after; that's the loop. Four. A real effect shows up in two of three numbers, not just the one you hoped would move. Five. *Inconclusive* is a valid outcome and means run another fourteen days, not panic.

[gentle] The reflection prompt. *What's the very first fourteen-day experiment you'll run, and on what date does it end?* [pause]

[warm] If you can answer that question with a date — you've finished the course. [wry, slowly] That's normal. Run the experiment on yourself anyway. [pause] See you in the capstone.
