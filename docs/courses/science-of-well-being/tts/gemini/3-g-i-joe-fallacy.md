### Speech block 1 — *Opening: the uncomfortable lesson*  *(target 0:00 – 1:30)*

[Indian-English accent, calm Bengaluru lecturer tone] [wry, slowly] You have now read two lessons of psychology. [pause] Statistically — this changes your behavior by roughly nothing.

[dry] Welcome to the most uncomfortable lesson in the course.

[matter-of-fact] Knowledge transfer is not the same as behavior change. The gap is the entire game. And the gap is bridgeable, mechanically, with four parts. The bad news is that *you* will skip at least one of them on the first attempt. [chuckles]

[firm] Today we install one mental model — the planner versus the doer — and one named bug, the *G.I. Joe Fallacy.* Then we walk through a four-part rewire that turns intent into a behavior the rest of you will actually follow.

[warm] Quick framing. Most lessons that try to change behavior fail because they aim at the planner. The planner is the part of you that knows. We're going to aim, instead, at the doer. The part of you that *does.* They are not the same person. [pause] In fact they barely speak.

### Speech block 2 — *Mental model: planner vs doer*  *(target 1:30 – 4:00)*

[curious] Picture two systems sharing a skull.

[matter-of-fact] On the left, the planner. The planner watches documentaries, has a Notion full of habits, knows the right answer, has read this course twice. The planner has an excellent vocabulary and almost no calluses.

[wry] On the right, the doer. The doer orders biryani at eleven thirty at night, scrolls Instagram for forty-five minutes when you needed five, reaches for the phone when the alarm rings. The doer has not read this course. The doer has not been *invited* to read this course.

[firm] The planner can read a hundred books on happiness without the doer ever picking up the memo.

[emphatic, slowly] Behavior change — is the work — of getting the doer — to receive a single, well-formatted memo.

[gentle] That's the entire skill. Most attempts at behavior change are essentially the planner shouting louder at the doer through a closed door, while the doer plays music, and we call that "trying harder." It does not work. It has never worked. It will not work tomorrow.

### Speech block 3 — *Move 1: name the bug*  *(target 4:00 – 7:00)*

[firm] First named move. The *G.I. Joe Fallacy.*

[matter-of-fact] G.I. Joe was a cartoon. The tagline at the end of every episode was, [slowly] *"Now you know — and knowing is half the battle."* [pause]

[wry] Laurie Santos, a psychologist at Yale who teaches the most-popular course at that university, formalized the bug. In real behavior change, knowing is closer to *ten percent* of the battle. The other ninety percent is friction, cues, accountability, and environment design.

[dry] Proof you've already lived. Every smoker who knows smoking is bad. Every developer who knows sleep matters. Every founder who knows they should not check email at seven a.m. [pause]

[gentle] Knowing is the *cheap* part. Doing is where the work, the design, and the actual life-changing happens.

[firm] Most self-improvement content focuses on the ten percent — what to know, what to think, what to read. The ninety percent — environment, friction, cues — is where almost all the leverage is. [emphatic] You have been investing in the wrong part of the system.

[switching to Hinglish, warm] Maa: *beta dahi kha, raat ko paani pee, subah jaldi uth, phone band kar de.*

[returning to English, dry] You've had this information for thirty years. Not one item followed. Was your mother wrong? No. Your mother was issuing memos to the planner. The doer never received them, because the doer's environment never changed. The doer is in front of a phone. There is no dahi in sight. Memo lost.

### Speech block 4 — *Move 2: the four-part rewire*  *(target 7:00 – 10:20)*

[firm] Second named move. The four-part rewire.

[matter-of-fact] This is the translation moment of the entire course. Take an English sentence — *"I want to walk every morning"* — and translate it into a four-part install spec the doer can actually run.

[curious] The four parts. Cue. Friction reduction. Tiny first step. Accountability. [emphatic] Drop one of the four and the whole machine free-spins.

[firm, slowly] One. *Cue.* When and where. Right after the alarm rings, before checking the phone. Specific. *"Sometime in the morning"* is not a cue. The cue must be a moment — a sound, a transition, a physical event — that can fire whether or not you remembered to plan today.

[matter-of-fact] Two. *Friction reduction.* Sleep in your walking clothes. Shoes by the door. Phone in another room so the doer cannot get distracted. Reduce, very specifically, the number of decisions and obstacles between the cue and the behavior. Each removed obstacle is worth more than any amount of motivation.

[wry] Three. *Tiny first step.* The goal is not "walk five kilometers." The goal is "put on shoes and step outside." [pause] Once outside, the walk happens by inertia. [chuckles] If your tiny first step requires willpower, it isn't tiny enough. Shrink it again.

[firm] Four. *Accountability and log.* Tell one person. Tick a calendar box. The streak is the dopamine, not the walk. Most people skip this part because it feels childish. The childishness is the point — the doer is, in many ways, a child.

[emphatic] All four required. Drop one and the machine free-spins.

[gentle] When the rewire fails — and your first attempts will fail — your instinct will be to *try harder tomorrow.* This is paying *willpower tax* to compensate for bad design. [firm] If you have failed the same habit three times, the bug is the environment. Not you. Stop paying the tax. Rebuild the design.

[switching to Hinglish, warm] Tu kabhi notice kiya — ghar pe rehne par tu apne aap subah uthta hai. Mummy kitchen mein cup ki awaaz, papa news dekh rahe hain. Cue is loud and external. [pause] Flat mein akela rehne par cue zero ho jaata hai. Phone alarm sirf snooze ka button ban jaata hai.

[returning to English, dry] You aren't lazy. You're under-cued. Replicate the kitchen. Build the environment. The discipline will follow.

### Speech block 5 — *Try it and reflect*  *(target 10:20 – 13:00)*

[warm] Time to run the rewire on a real habit.

[firm] Take one habit you have repeatedly failed to install. Pick a specific one. Don't pick a general category like "be healthier"; pick a specific behavior like "do twenty pushups in the morning" or "read for thirty minutes before bed." [pause]

[matter-of-fact] Specify all four parts. Embarrassingly specific.

[brisk] One. Cue. When, exactly. Where, exactly. Two. Friction. List three specific obstacles you will remove tonight, before the cue ever fires. Three. Tiny first step. The version that cannot fail. Four. Accountability. The exact person you'll text, the exact format of the ping.

[wry] Example. *I want to journal at night.* Cue: right after I plug in my phone to charge at ten forty-five p.m. Friction: notebook and pen kept on the charging pad. Same notebook — no system shopping. Tiny first step: write one sentence. Even *"today was fine"* counts. Accountability: text one friend a thumbs-up emoji each night I do it. Nothing if I skip. Ten skips and they call me.

[firm] That is what a real install spec looks like. If yours has any vague language — "sometime," "regularly," "I'll try" — go back and tighten it.

[brisk] Recap. Move one — the G.I. Joe Fallacy. Knowing is roughly ten percent. Move two — the four-part rewire. Cue, friction reduction, tiny first step, accountability. All four. Always.

[firm] Five takeaways. [pause] One. Knowing is roughly ten percent of the battle; the cartoon was wrong about the ratio. Two. Behavior change has four parts: cue, friction reduction, tiny step, accountability. Three. If you have failed a habit three times, the bug is the environment, not you. Four. Willpower is a tax; environment is a permanent rebate. Five. Most failures are missing only one of the four parts — diagnose, don't restart.

[gentle] Reflection prompt. *Which habit have you failed at three or more times that, looked at honestly, has zero environmental support?* [pause] Sit with that one for a minute.

[warm] Lesson four is next. We finally get to the things that *do* move the setpoint. [wry] The two cheat codes are coming.
