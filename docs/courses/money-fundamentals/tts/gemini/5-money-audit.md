### Speech block 3 — *Move 1: the three-number stack*  *(target 3:40 – 6:50)*

[firm] Move one. The three-number stack. We will install each one with the exact calculation method.

[matter-of-fact] Number one — *net worth.* What you own minus what you owe.

Assets — checking accounts, savings accounts, fixed deposits, mutual fund corpus, EPF balance, PPF balance, equity holdings, gold holdings, real estate at conservative current market value. Liabilities — outstanding home loan, car loan, credit card balances if not fully paid, personal loans. Subtract liabilities from assets. That single number is your net worth.

Update it once a month. Last Sunday of the month. Five minutes. Open each account, write the balance, sum it up.

[curious] One specific thing not to include — your *salary*. Salary is income, not net worth. The number changes when assets grow or liabilities shrink. Salary is the input that creates the change. They are different metrics.

[matter-of-fact] Number two — *savings rate.* Percentage of monthly income that did not get spent.

Calculation. Take your monthly take-home salary. Subtract everything that left the account that month — rent, groceries, EMIs, utilities, food delivery, subscriptions, transport, fun spending, gifts. Divide what is left by the take-home. Multiply by hundred. That is your savings rate for the month.

[dry] If the answer is single-digit, that is information. If the answer is negative — meaning you spent more than you earned and the difference came from credit card or loan — that is also information. Both answers tell you exactly what to fix and how urgently. Numbers do the diagnosis. The diagnosis is brutal because the numbers are honest.

[firm] Target ranges. Single income, twenties — aim for twenty percent. Single income, thirties with no dependents — aim for thirty. Dual income — aim for forty. These are *aim for* numbers. Hitting them takes a year or two of incremental adjustments. The number going up year over year is what matters more than the absolute level.

[matter-of-fact] Number three — *burn.* Monthly essential expenses. Rent, groceries, EMIs, utilities, insurance premiums, basic transport, parents' contributions if applicable.

Update it quarterly. Burn does not change month to month. It changes when you move flats, take on a new EMI, or have a major life event. Quarterly is the right cadence.

[curious] One use of burn we already covered in lesson two — multiply by six to get your emergency-fund target. The other use — *runway*. Divide your liquid net worth by burn. The answer is how many months you can survive with zero income. That number is the most psychologically powerful one in your entire financial life.

[switching to Hinglish, warm] Burn ka real meaning yeh hai — *agar kal salary band ho jaye, kitne mahine survive kar sakte ho?* Yeh number jab tu calculate karega, do mein se ek baat hogi — ya woh number bahut kam hai, jisme tu *uncomfortable* feel karega — ya bahut zyada hai, jisme tu *free* feel karega. Dono cases mein decision tujhe milega. *Number ne diagnose kar liya, dawai bhi number ne hi suggest kara.* [chuckles]

[returning to English, dry] If your runway is one month, the prescription is build the buffer first. If your runway is twelve months, the prescription is allocate more aggressively to equity because you can afford the volatility. The number tells you exactly what to do next. The number is not optional.

### Speech block 4 — *Move 2: run a 14-day single-variable experiment*  *(target 6:50 – 9:20)*

[firm] Move two. The fourteen-day single-variable experiment.

[matter-of-fact] Tracking the three numbers tells you *where you are*. Experiments tell you *what to change*. Without the experiments, the numbers become wallpaper — looked at, but not acted on.

[curious] The protocol. Pick *one* variable in your money life. Change *only* that variable. Run for fourteen days. Compare the three numbers — specifically savings rate and discretionary spending — to the previous fourteen days. Decide — keep the change, or revert.

[firm] Three rules. *One — single variable.* If you change two things at once, you cannot tell which one worked. *Two — fourteen days minimum.* Less than that is noise. *Three — write down the hypothesis before the experiment starts.* What you expect to happen. Then check what actually happened. The gap between expectation and result is where the learning lives.

[matter-of-fact] Three example experiments worth running.

Experiment one. *Cancel one subscription you barely use.* Hypothesis — savings rate goes up by zero point three percent. Run for fourteen days. Did you miss it? If no, keep it cancelled. Repeat next month with another one.

Experiment two. *Switch one daily spend to a weekly spend.* Daily cappuccino becomes weekend cappuccino, office coffee on weekdays. Hypothesis — savings rate up one percent, no change in actual happiness. Run for fourteen days.

Experiment three. *Increase your equity SIP by ₹2,000 per month.* Hypothesis — net cash available drops by ₹2,000, no change in lifestyle quality, savings rate up two percent. Run for one full month — this one needs longer because monthly bills bunch unpredictably.

[dry] Most of these experiments will end with *yes, keep the change*. A few will end with *no, revert, that mattered more than I thought*. Both answers are wins. The wins compound. Twenty experiments a year — a third of which become permanent — is a savings rate that climbs five to ten percentage points over two years without any single dramatic life change.

[switching to Hinglish, warm] Indian household har Diwali pe ek annual review hota hai. *Yeh saal hum ne kya kharcha kara, kya saved? Next saal ka plan?* [pause] Yeh experiment loop exactly same hai but at a smaller scale. Diwali audit, scaled to weekly. Fourteen-day experiment. Tracking three numbers. Mummy ne kabhi PowerPoint nahi banayi. Ek register, paanch entries, decision. *Same outcome. Better discipline.* [chuckles]

[returning to English, gentle] You saw this system at home twenty-five years ago. The Diwali review — *what did we save, what did we spend, what's the plan for next year* — was the annual version. The weekly version is the same loop, smaller cadence. The discipline existed in your household; what's missing is the cadence. Install it.
