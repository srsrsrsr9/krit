The principle
Time is the multiplier.
Money is just what gets multiplied.
— What the chart was showing you the whole time

The compounding formula is FV = P × (1 + r)t. Notice which variable lives in the exponent: t. Time. Doubling your savings doubles the result. Doubling the time you let it run, at 11%, multiplies the result by about 8×.

Most financial advice tells you to "save more." That's true but secondary. The single decision that dominates everything else is when you start. Year-one ₹10,000 invested at 22 becomes ₹947,000 by 65. The same ₹10,000 invested at 35 becomes ₹239,000. The 22-year-old version did 4× as much for you — for the same rupees out of pocket.

The trap

Why doesn't everyone do this?

We discount the future. Not metaphorically — literally. Daniel Kahneman, Richard Thaler, and the behavioural-economics generation showed that humans value a rupee today disproportionately more than a rupee tomorrow. The technical name is hyperbolic discounting: the discount we apply to a future reward grows much faster as the time grows than rational economic models predict.

Translated to a 22-year-old: your future self at 65 feels less real than a stranger. You will not save for them the way you'd save for yourself, because they are not yet you. Brain scans bear this out — when subjects imagine their distant-future selves, their brains light up the regions for thinking about other people, not the regions for thinking about themselves (Hershfield et al., Stanford, 2011).

That's why the compound interest argument never works on its own. The maths is fine. The maths is just true. The block is that the person who benefits doesn't exist yet in your head.

The actual move

Automate the transfer. Set a standing instruction the day your salary hits to move your-committed-rate% into an index fund SIP before you ever see the money. The Engineer A version of you isn't a hero — they just removed the decision. Friction at the top of the funnel beats willpower every time.

Three concrete things you can do in the next hour

  1. Open a brokerage account if you don't have one. Zerodha, Groww, Kuvera — all free, all take 15 minutes online.
  2. Set up a SIP in any Nifty 50 index fund. The exact fund matters less than the fact that the SIP exists. UTI, Nippon, ICICI — all charge under 0.2% expense ratio. The "best" one this year may not be next year; the SIP itself is what compounds.
  3. Increase the SIP every year by your raise. Your future self thanks you; your present self never feels it.

That's the whole game. The chart you scrubbed above is the only argument. Everything else — asset allocation, tax shelters, debt funds, risk tolerance — sits on top of start early, automate, increase with income. Most of it doesn't matter until you have the base.
