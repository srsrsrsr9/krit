Claude Design: Mockups, Copy, Critique

A junior designer who has read every book and shipped nothing

notes by Karthik ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Designers come to Claude with two opposite expectations and both are wrong. The first expectation: *Claude will generate beautiful mockups for me*.

Mental model: junior designer with the entire library memorised
Picture the most well-read junior designer in the country. They can quote Krug on usability, Wroblewski on forms, Norman on signifiers, Saffer on micro-interactions. They have never run a real user test. They have never argued with engineering about feasibility. They have never watched a real user fumble through their flow. So they are useful for vocabulary, principles, and critique against named criteria. They are useless when the question is *'what would actually work for our users in this specific context'*. That is your job, always.

Move 1: Critique, Don't Generate

  • The single highest-leverage Claude design move is this: **show your mockup to Claude with a named user, a named goal, and a named primary action; ask Claude to identify the three weakest decisions before suggesting fixes**. Notice everything that constrains the answer.
  • Name the user: Specific: '38-year-old wholesaler in Surat, on a 6-inch Android, one-handed, noisy market'.
  • Name the goal: Specific: 'place a repeat order in under 90 seconds with no surprises at the price line'.
  • Name the primary action: Specific: 'tap reorder card, confirm shipping address, pay via UPI'.
  • Attach the mockup: Image upload to Claude.ai, with a one-line caption of what each region of the screen is.
  • Ask the critique: 'Find the three weakest decisions in this screen given the user, goal and primary action. Then propose a fix for each. Do not redesign.'
  • Triage the response: Accept what fits, reject what doesn't. Note the rejections — they reveal where Claude lacks context. Iterate.

Move 2: The Iterative-Tightening Loop

  • Designs do not get good in one round. They get good in **3-5 rounds where each round tightens one specific constraint**.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: confusing well-read with experienced: Claude can quote Norman, Krug, Wroblewski. Claude cannot tell you whether the *Add to Cart* button on your screen is in the right place for your users. Reading a thousand books on swimming does not teach you to swim. The trap is treating Claude's authoritative tone as authority. The defense: every Claude design suggestion gets matched against either real user data or your own shipped experience. If neither exists, the suggestion is a hypothesis, not an answer.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Claude is a junior designer who has read every book and shipped nothing — bring the taste, take the vocabulary.
Critique-not-generate: name the user, name the goal, name the primary action, ask for the 3 weakest decisions.
Iterative-tightening: 5 rounds with one constraint each beats 1 round with everything mashed together.
Claude does not generate images; use it to sharpen the brief and constrain a real image model's prompt.
Authoritative tone is not authority; every design suggestion is a hypothesis until matched against real user data.
