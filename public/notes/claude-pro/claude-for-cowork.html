Claude as a Coworker — Slack, Email, Meetings

A draft engine, not the sender

notes by Meera ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Cowork is the use case where Claude looks easy and is actually the most dangerous. Unlike code, where a broken build screams at you, a Claude-drafted Slack message that subtly misrepresents what your CFO said only fails 48 hours later when the CFO sees it in a thread.

Mental model: Claude is a draft engine, not the sender
Imagine an unpaid intern who can ghostwrite anything in 8 seconds. They cannot read tone in your team. They do not know that Anjali takes 'circle back' as a soft no. They do not know your CEO hates exclamation marks. They will produce a clean, generic, reasonable draft. Your job, every single time, is to read it, reshape it, sign it, and **send it from your own keyboard**. Claude is the printer. You are the journalist.

Move 1: The No-Send-Without-Review Rule

  • Make it a rule, not a vibe. **No message generated by Claude leaves your account without a full read-through and at least one substantive edit**.
  • Read the email: You read it first. Note the actual ask, the tone, what you can and cannot promise.
  • Bullet your reply: Three to five bullets in your own words. The bullets are what only you know.
  • Ask Claude to expand: Paste the original, your bullets, and your house style. Ask for one draft, not three.
  • Read every line: Look for: invented commitments, wrong tone for the relationship, AI-isms, exclamation marks.
  • Cut at least 30 percent: First drafts are always 30 percent too long. Cut decisively.
  • Send from your client: Copy into Outlook or Front. Do not let Claude or any agent send. Your name, your send.

Move 2: The Bullet-to-Paragraph Expander

  • Ninety percent of professional writing is *'I have the points; I need them in flowing prose for an audience'*. Status updates, board notes, performance reviews, postmortems, RFP responses, even most LinkedIn posts.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: the asymmetry of small wins and big losses: Cowork is a domain where 95 percent of Claude-drafted messages are fine and 5 percent are catastrophic — wrong tone to a board member, fabricated commitment to a customer, leaked confidential context, AI-prefix in a CEO DM. Treating each message individually leads to *'it's been fine so far'* complacency. The right framing: **the cost of one bad message is asymmetric**. One wrong message to a regulator can cost ₹2 crore. One wrong message to a candidate can lose them. One wrong message to your CEO can make you the punchline for a quarter. The review gate exists for the 5 percent.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Claude is a draft engine, not the sender. The send is always you, from your keyboard.
No-send-without-review: full read-through plus at least one substantive edit, every time.
The bullet-to-paragraph expander is the highest-frequency cowork move; you hold judgement, Claude holds prose.
Pin 3-5 of your own past examples in a Project so Claude drafts arrive in your voice.
The cost of one bad message is asymmetric; the review gate exists for the 5 percent that matter.
