What AI Actually Does for a Business

Two curves shifted; everything else is just shopping

notes by Aanya ✏️

~ 14 min read ~

Your CEO said the company is 'AI-first now.' The all-hands clapped. Engineering went back to their laptops.

Mental model: AI shifted two curves, not one wave
Forget 'AI is a wave.' Waves do not give you decision rules.

Move 1 — The 80/20 capability lens

  • Most of your team's AI ideas are not bad; they are *misallocated*. Eighty percent of them are about tasks where AI is technically possible but commercially uninteresting; the other twenty percent are about tasks where AI just became commercially possible for the first time.
  • Step 1 — The English question: Director of CX says: 'We should use AI for customer support.' Sounds reasonable. Means almost nothing.
  • Step 2 — Curve check (capability): Ask: what is now feasible? 'Drafting responses to ambiguous tickets in passable English' became feasible in 2024. 'Resolving complex billing disputes end-to-end' did not.
  • Step 3 — Curve check (cost): Ask: at what cost? Drafting at ~₹0.40 per ticket vs an agent at ₹38. Ratio is 95:1, well past the 'commercially interesting' threshold.
  • Step 4 — Scope the surface: Restate as: 'Draft first-response for tier-1 tickets in English-only categories. Agent edits before send. Tier-2 unchanged.' This is now a feature spec, not a slogan.
  • Step 5 — Tag the assumption: Write down: 'We assume our tier-1 ticket mix is >60% English-only and that draft acceptance is >55%. We will know by day 45.' This goes into the assumption ledger you will build in lesson 5.

Move 2 — The 'what's now feasible that wasn't' question

  • Move 2 is a habit, not a framework. Every time someone in your company proposes an AI feature, you ask one question, in exactly these words: > *"What is now feasible that was not feasible 18 months ago — and how do we know?"* The phrasing matters.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: shiny demo, no measurable outcome: The single most common failure pattern in 2024-26 leader AI programs: a team builds a demo, shows it at all-hands, gets applause, ships nothing.

Takeaways (memorize these)

AI is two curves moving against each other — cost falling, capability rising. Strategy lives where they cross.
Most AI ideas at your company are theatre; the 80/20 lens kills them in one meeting.
Ask one question — 'what is now feasible that was not feasible 18 months ago, and how do we know?' — and ask it every time.
A demo without a cohort-scoped metric is a screensaver; never let a demo close without one.
If your strategy fails the mummy-aunty test, your board will not save you.
