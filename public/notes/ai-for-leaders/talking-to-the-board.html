Talking to Boards and Executives About AI

Honest timelines, named killcriteria, no 18-month vapor

notes by Ishaan ✏️

~ 14 min read ~

Most VPs lose the board on AI by promising too much. The board doesn't punish honest tradeoffs; it punishes vapor that misses by 18 months.

Mental model: the assumption ledger
A board ask is a forecast. A forecast without an assumption ledger is a wish. Your job is to write down: *what specifically must be true for this to work, by when will we know, what's the rollback if it doesn't.*

Move 1 — Cost-benefit framing

  • Every AI bet has four numbers worth declaring up-front: 1. **Investment** — actual rupees and people across 90 days.
  • 1. Frame the bet: Specific outcome with a date. Not 'reduce CAC'; 'reduce paid CAC by ≥10% by Sep 30, with no LTV regression'.
  • 2. Investment line: Engineers, PM, money, 90-day window. Concrete.
  • 3. Confidence note: Your honest probability. 'We think 50-60%; here's why.'
  • 4. Kill criterion: By when, and on what number. 'If at day 45 CAC hasn't moved ≥3%, we kill this and reallocate.'
  • 5. The rollback: What happens if it fails. Capital and people freed up; what do they do next.

Move 2 — The rollback narrative

  • Boards trust executives who plan for failure. The rollback narrative — what happens if this bet fails, who absorbs the cost, what gets reallocated — is the single most underrated trust-building move with a board.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: hiding the rollback: Most VPs think hiding the failure case makes the pitch stronger. It doesn't.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Board AI memos need four lines: investment, expected outcome, confidence, kill criterion
The rollback narrative is the highest-trust move you can make with a board
'AI-first' / 'transformation' / 'parity' are board allergens — replace with dated commitments
Pre-commit your kill criterion on slide 3, not when the metric flatlines at month 5
One-breath sentence test: 'X by Y, killing if Z'
