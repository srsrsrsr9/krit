Real eval gates beat policy docs every audit, every quarter

Your Compliance head walks in with a 47-page AI ethics policy. Legal has signed off.

Mental model: real eval gates beat policy docs, every time
A **policy doc** describes what the company aspires to. It contains nouns: 'fairness', 'accountability', 'transparency.' It can be 47 pages long and approved by Legal. It blocks nothing.

Move 1 — The kill switch

  • A kill switch is the smallest, cheapest, highest-leverage piece of AI governance you can build. The complete spec fits in three lines: 1.
  • Step 1 — The English question: CEO in a postmortem: 'We should be able to turn it off.' Everyone nods. Nothing changes.
  • Step 2 — Pick the named on-call: Translate to: 'Priya, Anika, Rohan rotate weekly. PagerDuty escalation. Backup: head of platform.' If you cannot name three humans, you have a one-person dependency.
  • Step 3 — Pick the time-to-disable: '15 minutes from page to feature-flag-off.' This forces engineering to wire a feature flag to a single button — not a deploy, not a config push, a flag.
  • Step 4 — Pick the fallback: 'Form-based claim entry; estimated processing time 24 hrs; show banner explaining the AI flow is paused.' If the fallback is 'show error', users will assume the company is broken.
  • Step 5 — Run a fire drill: Quarterly. The on-call pulls the switch on a low-traffic feature, you measure time-to-disable. If you have never run a drill, you do not have a kill switch; you have a screenshot of one.

Move 2 — What to write down

  • There are exactly four artefacts that must be written down before any AI feature touches money, identity, or health. Anything more is bureaucracy; anything less is theatre.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: policy doc, no enforcement: The most common, most expensive failure mode in 2024-26 AI governance: a polished policy document that is approved, circulated, never converted to enforceable artefacts, and never updated until the next incident.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Real eval gates beat policy docs because they are testable; anything that cannot fail cannot govern.
A kill switch is three lines: who, in how many minutes, with what fallback. If you cannot answer all three, you do not have one.
The four artefacts that gate every sensitive AI feature: eval card, kill switch spec, logging schema, named owner. Four pages, four signatures.
If a line of policy cannot convert to an eval / log / kill / owner artefact, delete it. The doc collapses; the program improves.
Auditors do not measure policy by weight; they measure it by the last enforced action.
