Measure Your Mind

If you don't track it, you can't tell whether any of this worked

notes by Ishaan ✏️

~ 16 min read ~

You track your weight. You probably track your steps.

Mental model: just-enough observability
Treat your well-being like a small, well-instrumented system. You don't need a dashboard with 40 metrics; you need 3 numbers, captured cheaply, looked at weekly.

Move 1 — The 3-number stack

  • There are exactly three numbers worth tracking weekly. Skip any of them and you will misdiagnose yourself.

Move 2 — Run a 14-day intervention

  • Tracking is necessary; tracking alone is not the experiment. The experiment is: pick one intervention from this course, run it for 14 days, compare your check-in numbers before and after.
  • Where you are now — You walked in believing happiness was a feeling that happened to you. You are walking out with: a model (thermostat), four named bugs (miswanting, arrival, intensity arms race, G.I. Joe), two cheat codes (structured gratitude, engineered connection), and a 14-day experimental loop.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: the dashboard delusion: You can spend a fun weekend building a Notion dashboard with 22 metrics, automated graphs, and a mood emoji column. You will check it twice and then never again.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Three numbers — PANAS, Cantril ladder, sleep — are enough; more is procrastination
Positive and negative affect are not opposites; track both
One intervention, 14 days, before-after; that's the loop
A real effect shows up in 2 of 3 numbers, not just the one you hoped would move
'Inconclusive' is a valid outcome and means run another 14 days, not panic
