Why Nothing Stays Special

The thermostat in your skull does not care about your raise

notes by Vikrant ✏️

~ 14 min read ~

You assume good things keep feeling good. They do not.

Mental model: the happiness thermostat
Think of happiness like a thermostat set somewhere around 7 out of 10. External events push it briefly up (new job, vacation, biryani) or briefly down (breakup, food poisoning, a passive-aggressive Slack message). The thermostat then quietly pulls it back to 7.

Move 1 — The adaptation curve

  • Brickman's 1978 study compared lottery winners (recent) against paraplegics (recent accident) against controls. **Eighteen months later, all three groups reported similar happiness levels.** The win and the catastrophe both faded.
  • T+0 days — Unboxing: Maximum delta from baseline. You take photographs of a box. Mood: 9.5 out of 10.
  • T+3 days — Showing off: Still well above baseline. You bring it up in unrelated conversations. Mood: 9 out of 10.
  • T+14 days — Becoming furniture: Roughly half-decayed. You stop noticing it on the table. Mood: 8 out of 10.
  • T+45 days — Pure utility: Back to baseline. The thing now exists only as a tool. Mood: 7 out of 10.
  • T+90 days — Mild irritant: Below baseline if it has any flaws. You start eyeing the next model. Mood: 6.7 out of 10.

Move 2 — Variety beats intensity

  • If adaptation is the enemy, the cheap counter is **variety**. Five different small pleasures (coffee with a friend Tuesday, a 4 km walk Wednesday, a new playlist Thursday) consistently beat one expensive intense pleasure (a five-star dinner, once) on weekly happiness measures.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: the intensity arms race: When you feel the bump fading, your instinct is to *increase the dose*. Bigger phone, fancier dinner, longer vacation. This works for about one cycle and then your hedonic budget collapses.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Hedonic adaptation is plumbing, not philosophy; you cannot will it away
Variety beats intensity; deprivation beats variety
The intensity arms race is the most expensive way to be only briefly happier
Symmetric adaptation is also why most bad days fade faster than you fear
The strongest single re-sensitizer is short, bounded deprivation
