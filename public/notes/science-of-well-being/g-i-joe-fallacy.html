Knowing Is Half The Battle (And Half Is Almost Nothing)

Why everyone in this lesson already failed lessons one and two

notes by Meera ✏️

~ 15 min read ~

You have now read two lessons of psychology. Statistically, this changes your behavior by roughly nothing.

Mental model: the planner and the doer
Think of your mind as two systems: the **planner** (knows the right answer, watches documentaries, has a Notion full of habits) and the **doer** (orders biryani at 11 pm).

Move 1 — Name the bug: the G.I. Joe Fallacy

  • The cartoon's tagline was *"Now you know — and knowing is half the battle."* Laurie Santos formalized the bug: in real behavior change, knowing is closer to **10%** of the battle. The other 90% is friction, cues, accountability, and environment design.
  • 1. Cue (when + where): Right after the alarm rings, before checking the phone. Specific. 'Sometime in the morning' is not a cue.
  • 2. Friction reduction: Sleep in your walking clothes. Shoes by the door. Phone in another room so the doer cannot get distracted.
  • 3. Tiny first step: Goal is not 'walk 5 km.' Goal is 'put on shoes and step outside.' Once outside, the walk happens by inertia.
  • 4. Accountability + log: Tell one person. Tick a calendar box. The streak is the dopamine, not the walk.

Move 2 — Translation moment: turn intent into a 4-part behavior

  • The fix is mechanical, not motivational. Any behavior you actually want to install needs four parts.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: the willpower tax: When the rewire fails, your instinct is to *try harder tomorrow*. This is paying willpower tax to compensate for bad design. Willpower is finite and unreliable; environment is permanent.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Knowing is roughly 10% of the battle; the cartoon was wrong about the ratio
Behavior change has 4 parts: cue, friction reduction, tiny step, accountability
If you have failed a habit 3 times, the bug is the environment, not you
Willpower is a tax; environment is a permanent rebate
Most failures are missing only one of the four parts — diagnose, don't restart
