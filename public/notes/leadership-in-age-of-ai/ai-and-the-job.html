What AI Does to Knowledge Work

Tasks change fast, jobs change slow, and the gap is where leadership lives

notes by Aanya ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Most AI plans your CEO has read on the flight back from a conference are wrong in the same way. They treat AI as something that automates *jobs*.

Mental model: a job is a stack of tasks, not a monolith
Hold this picture. A *task* is a 30-minute, scoped unit of work with a defined output: write the status memo, scrape three competitors, summarise the call. A *job* is the stack of 80 to 200 such tasks plus the human who is accountable when the stack misbehaves.

Move 1 — The 70-20-10 split

  • Look at any knowledge worker's week and the hours land in three buckets. About **70% is purely human work**: judgment, ambiguous calls, relationship maintenance, the cost of saying no, the politics of saying yes.
  • Step 1 — List 20 tasks: Pick the team. List the 20 most-frequent tasks anyone on the team did last week. Concrete: 'write Monday status memo', 'review junior's deck', 'pull churn numbers'. Not 'do strategy'.
  • Step 2 — Tag each task: Tag each as Human / With-AI / AI-only based on whether AI can do 80% of it well today. Be honest, not aspirational. 'Strategic thinking' is not a task. 'Summarise the strategy doc into 5 bullets' is.
  • Step 3 — Pick the 20%: Look at the With-AI bucket. That is your redesign target. Not the 70%, not the 10%. The middle. Most leaders try to redesign the 10% (which is already automated) or the 70% (which is not movable yet).
  • Step 4 — Choose ONE workflow: Of the With-AI tasks, pick the ONE that runs at least weekly and has a clear quality signal. That's your pilot. Not three workflows. One.
  • Step 5 — Redesign the ritual: Don't add an 'AI step' to the existing ritual. Redesign the ritual: who produces the artifact, who reviews it, what the new failure mode is, how you'll catch it. The license was the easy part.

Move 2 — The 'what stays human' question

  • The 70-20-10 split tells you what shifts. The harder question is what *doesn't*.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: 'automate everything, replace people': Every quarter, some leader you respect will say a version of *"AI does X, so we don't need people for X anymore."* Watch for the slip from task to job. AI does *the writing task*; it does not do *the writer's job*. AI handles *first-pass review*; it does not do *the engineer's job*.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Tasks change in months; jobs change in years; lead in the gap.
About 47% of knowledge-work tasks are 80%+ automatable, but about 80% of jobs are 0% automatable; both numbers are simultaneously true.
The 70-20-10 split: 70% human, 20% human-with-AI, 10% AI-only — redesign the 20%, not the 10%.
Three things stay human: accountability, calibration to specific people, ambiguity arbitration; design every role around this 30%.
'Automate everything, replace people' is the most expensive trap in 2026 — it eats the seed corn that grows your seniors.
