Continuous Learning as a Leadership Move

Weekly demos and public mistakes beat the annual L&D budget every time

notes by Karthik ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Your L&D budget for 2026 is not actually a learning budget. It is a feel-good budget that produces certificates, attendance reports, and the occasional inspirational PDF.

Mental model: learning sprints, not annual L&D budget
Stop thinking of L&D as a budget you spend once a year. Start thinking of it as a sprint cadence the team runs continuously, like engineering runs two-week sprints. **Short** — one new thing per fortnight, not seven things per quarter. **Specific** — one team's actual workflow, not a generic course catalogue. **Owned** — a named person who runs the demo, not a vendor.

Move 1 — The public-mistake protocol

  • If you only adopt one ritual from this lesson, adopt this one. The **public-mistake protocol** is a 15-minute, dated, recurring slot where a senior person on the team stands up and says: *Last fortnight, I did this with AI.
  • Step 1 — Senior books the slot: Calendar: every Friday 4:30pm, 30 minutes, recurring. Senior leader (you) goes first for at least the first 6 weeks. Don't delegate this; if you delegate, you've signalled it's not real.
  • Step 2 — Senior shares first: One specific incident. What you did with AI, what went wrong, what it cost (in money or time or trust), what you'll change. Be specific to the point of discomfort. Vague stories signal vague intent.
  • Step 3 — Open the room: Two slots for anyone else. First fortnight, nobody volunteers; that's expected. Don't fill the silence; sit in it. Second fortnight, one volunteer. Sixth fortnight, fight for slots.
  • Step 4 — Capture the artifact: Each shared mistake gets a 5-line entry in a shared 'Mistakes Wiki' — date, owner, AI tool, failure mode, change made. The wiki is the proof the protocol is compounding. Without it, it's confession; with it, it's curriculum.
  • Step 5 — Reference the wiki in onboarding: New hires read the last 8 weeks of mistakes in their first week. This is the fastest way to seed the protocol's norms in someone who joined after you started it. Skip this and the protocol doesn't survive a year of attrition.

Move 2 — The weekly demo

  • The second move pairs cleanly with the first: the **weekly demo**. Every Wednesday afternoon, 25 minutes, one team member demos *one new thing they did with AI this week*.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: L&D = compliance training: The most common L&D failure mode in 2026 is treating AI training as a compliance line item. Module assigned, video watched, certificate issued, attendance logged. Compliance is necessary for things like data-handling and harassment policy; it is *useless* for capability building.

Takeaways (memorize these)

AI tooling has a 6-week knowledge half-life; annual L&D cycles cannot keep up by design.
Sprint L&D produces capability above a steady-state line; annual training spikes once and decays.
Public-mistake protocol works only when the senior goes first; delegating to L&D destroys the mechanism.
Weekly 25-minute demos with wiki artifacts compound faster than any external training your CHRO will buy.
If the only artifact your L&D produces is a completion certificate, you're running compliance, not learning.
