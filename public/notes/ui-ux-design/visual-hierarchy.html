Visual Hierarchy: How the Eye Travels

The eye is a lazy heuristic engine; your job is to bait it on purpose

notes by Aanya ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Open any SaaS dashboard in India built between 2019 and 2024. Count the buttons that are competing to be 'the' button on the screen.

Mental model: the eye is a lazy heuristic engine
The visual system burns calories. To save them, it picks shortcuts: scan for the brightest, the most isolated, the most differently-coloured patch on the screen, and start there. **You don't get to override this. You get to design with it.** A screen that fights the heuristic loses; a screen that bait-and-switches it wins.

Move 1 — Contrast as hierarchy

  • **Contrast** is the cheapest, fastest, most reliable way to set hierarchy. Not size.
  • Step 1 — Pick one primary action per screen: If you have two primary buttons, you have one primary button and one regret. Decide which is the answer to the screen's question.
  • Step 2 — Mute everything else: Drop secondary buttons to outline-only or text-only. Push body text to a 70% opacity grey. The job is to make the primary stand out by contrast, not by being louder.
  • Step 3 — Maximise the contrast on the primary: Filled, dark, branded. Aim for 7:1 contrast against its background. If the rest of the screen is muted, even a small button dominates.
  • Step 4 — Squint test: Blur your eyes or shrink the screen to 25%. The primary action should still be the first thing you can identify. If not, you have a contrast failure.

Move 2 — Position as weight

  • After contrast comes **position**. The eye has priors — left-to-right reading, top-to-bottom flow, the F-pattern on text-heavy pages, the Z-pattern on landing pages.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: bigger is hierarchy: The instinct of every untrained designer is to make the thing they care about **bigger**. Bigger heading, bigger button, bigger badge. The result is two things competing on size, neither winning on contrast, and a screen that feels shouty and confused. **Hierarchy is a relative property of the whole screen, not an absolute property of one element.** Most hierarchy fixes happen by making other things smaller or quieter, not by making the hero thing louder.

Takeaways (memorize these)

The eye is a lazy heuristic engine. It scans for the loudest, most isolated patch first. Design with that, not against it.
Contrast outranks size. A small dark button on a pale screen beats a giant grey button every time.
One primary action per screen. Two primary buttons mean one primary button and one regret.
Position tie-breaks when contrast is constant. Top-right of the header or bottom-right of the card both win.
When a button doesn't 'pop', the answer is almost always to mute everything else, not to make the button louder.
