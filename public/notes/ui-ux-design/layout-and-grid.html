Layout and Grids — The Invisible Skeleton

A grid is a contract; spacing is the punctuation

notes by Vikrant ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Some screens feel calm. Other screens feel itchy.

Mental model: the grid is a contract
A grid is not a visual aesthetic; it is **a contract you sign with your future self and your engineers**. It says: 'every spacing decision in this product comes from this list of numbers — 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64. No exceptions without a written reason.' The reason a system feels deliberate is that it has fewer choices, not more. The grid is what removes the choices.

Move 1 — The 8-point spacing scale

  • Not because Apple did, not because Google did — because 8 has the cleanest factors of any small number that still gives you usable steps. Half of 8 is 4 (still on a pixel boundary).
  • Step 1 — Audit current spacing: Grep your CSS for px values. You will find dozens of unique numbers between 1 and 64. Most of them are within 2px of an 8-point step.
  • Step 2 — Define the scale as tokens: In Tailwind, this is mostly the default. Define spacing-1 through spacing-16 in your config, but keep the names usable: tight, snug, cozy, comfy, loose.
  • Step 3 — Replace per-component overrides: Sweep one screen at a time. The first three are slow; the next ten go fast because the patterns repeat.
  • Step 4 — Add a lint rule: Forbid arbitrary px values in className strings. The cost of slipping back is now visible in CI, not in code review.
  • Step 5 — Document the exceptions: Some places need a 6px badge gap. Fine — write down which and why, so the next person knows it was a decision, not an accident.

Move 2 — The gutter that earns its keep

  • Once your spacing scale is locked, the next decision is the **gutter** — the gap between columns in a multi-column layout. The default gutter on a 12-column desktop grid is 24 or 32 pixels.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: the 1px freelance: Every grid dies the same way. An engineer adds padding-15 because 'it looks slightly better than 16'. Three sprints later you have padding-13, padding-17, padding-22. None of them visibly wrong. Together: chaos. **The grid is a contract. The first off-scale value is the moment the contract dies.** Treat off-scale paddings the way you treat `console.log` in production: not catastrophic, but always a smell.

Takeaways (memorize these)

A grid is a contract. The whole win is the constraint, not the aesthetic.
Pick 8 as your base unit. The math is the reason: clean halves, quarters, and pixel-perfect rendering.
Five to seven steps cover everything. Most days you live between 16 and 32.
Gutters must exceed internal padding. Otherwise cards look glued together.
The grid dies the moment someone ships padding-15 'because it looks better'. Defend the contract.
