The Design Feedback Loop

Critique is the second design step, not the last

notes by Ishaan ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Most teams treat design critique as the last gate before shipping. The designer pushes a Figma link to Slack, four people drop unstructured opinions in the thread, the loudest one wins, and the work goes back for a 'revision'.

Mental model: critique is the second design step, not the last
Treat critique as **step two of the design process, not step ten**. The job of the first sketch is to become wrong on purpose so a critique can pull it toward right. Designers who polish before critique waste polish; teams that critique only at the end produce design that is locally optimal and globally weird. **Make the cost of being wrong cheap by inviting critique while the work is still ugly.**

Move 1 — The 3-question critique

  • Most critique fails because it has no structure. 'I don't like the colour' is not actionable; 'this could be cleaner' is not a critique, it's a vibe.
  • Min 0-2 — Show the work cold: No designer commentary, no walkthrough. Reviewers see the screen the way a user would. Silence is fine.
  • Min 2-5 — Question 1: primary action: Each reviewer points at what they think is the primary action. If you get more than one answer, you have a structural problem to fix before continuing.
  • Min 5-9 — Question 2: cold context: Pretend the user landed here from a deep link with no onboarding. Walk through what is unclear, missing, or assumed.
  • Min 9-13 — Question 3: anticipated pushback: Designer names the three most likely senior-design pushbacks. Reviewers add their own. Write them down.
  • Min 13-15 — Decisions: Three buckets: fix now, fix in next iteration, document and ship. No 'discuss further' — that's procrastination dressed up as care.

Move 2 — The spec-first PR

  • When design moves to engineering, most teams lose half the work. The Figma file gets copy-pasted into components, the 'why' evaporates, and three sprints later nobody remembers why the modal was 480px wide instead of 520.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: the polish-before-critique cycle: Designers want to show finished work; reviewers want to be invited to opinion. So designers polish for two days, then 'open up critique' on a thing that is too pretty to change. Reviewers feel rude pointing at structural problems and instead trade taste on colour and copy. **The polished design is the enemy of the good design.** Invite critique while the work is sketchy and the changes are cheap. The polish layer is the last 10%, not the first 90%.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Critique is the second design step, not the last. Invite it while changes are cheap.
The 3-question critique: what's the primary action, what breaks with no context, what would a cold reviewer push back on. In that order, every time.
If three reviewers give three different answers to 'what's the primary action', you have a hierarchy problem, not a critique problem.
The spec-first PR: three paragraphs of intent (what changed, why, what we're not doing) above the diff. Free, high-leverage, ritual.
End every critique with decisions in three buckets: fix now, fix later, document and ship. 'Discuss further' is procrastination.
