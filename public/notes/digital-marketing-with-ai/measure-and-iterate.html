Measuring What Matters in 2026

One north-star, a 14-day loop, and a kill criterion that survives Friday night

Open any marketing dashboard in 2026 and you'll find 47 widgets, 9 filters, 3 sparkline rails. Engagement.

Mental model: one north-star, attached to an action
A real north-star metric has three properties:

Move 1 — The 14-day campaign loop

  • The right cadence for marketing iteration in 2026 is 14 days. Not weekly (too noisy, churns the team), not quarterly (too slow, kills agility).
  • Day 0 — Pre-register: Launch AI-drafted Meta ads against current human-written control. Target: ₹40k spend per arm. North-star metric: paid-CAC over 14 days. Kill criterion: 'If by day 10, AI arm CAC is > ₹1,200 (current control is ₹847), we kill.' Scale: 'If AI arm CAC is < ₹720 by day 14 with n>4,000 conversions, we shift 60% of budget.'
  • Day 7 — Look: AI arm CAC at day 7: ₹980 (n=820 conversions). Control: ₹860. Note: AI is trending up but sample inside noise band. Don't act. Continue.
  • Day 10 — Kill check: AI arm CAC: ₹1,090 (n=1,400). Below the kill threshold of ₹1,200, but only barely. Continue running by the rule. Note: temptation to kill early ('it's clearly trending bad') is also an unauthorized override of the rule. Honor the threshold.
  • Day 14 — Decide: AI arm CAC: ₹1,140 (n=2,200). Control: ₹880. Result: AI arm is worse, but not catastrophically so. Decision: kill the AI arm; document the learning ('AI ads underperform our anchored human voice for this audience at this stage'); rerun the test on a different funnel stage where stakes per piece are lower.
  • Wrap — Decision Doc: Write the one-paragraph 'what we learned' note. Without it, the loop is just churn. With it, the team has a memory and the next experiment is sharper.

Move 2 — The kill criterion that holds

  • A kill criterion that survives Friday night at 11pm has four properties: 1. **Pre-registered.** Written before launch, sent to the team, dated.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: vanity metrics dashboard: The trap is identifiable in a sentence: **a dashboard is a vanity dashboard if no number on it has a kill threshold attached.** Every metric without a threshold is decoration. The dashboard might look beautiful, cost money, and update nightly via an AI pipeline; without thresholds, it cannot drive a single decision.

Takeaways (memorize these)

A north-star metric maps to economic outcome, moves on a 14-90 day timescale, and has a written kill threshold and scale threshold.
If no number on your dashboard has a kill threshold attached, the dashboard is decoration — not measurement.
Run on a 14-day campaign loop: pre-register day 0, look (don't act) day 7, kill-check day 10, decide day 14.
A kill criterion that survives Friday night at 11pm is pre-registered, specific, cheap to honor, and pre-emptively immune to the three excuses (more time, creative tweak, switch attribution).
Speed of learning beats speed of shipping; without the documented loop, you ship faster and learn nothing.
