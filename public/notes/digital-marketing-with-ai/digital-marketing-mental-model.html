Marketing Math, Then Marketing AI

Your CAC is not a creative problem

notes by Aanya ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

There are two tribes of marketers in 2026. Tribe A refuses to touch AI; their writers ship four blogs a week, their CAC is ₹847, and they're losing share to Tribe B.

Mental model: CAC/LTV first, AI second
Before any tool, any agency, any prompt: know two numbers.

Move 1 — The funnel-stage lens

  • Pick any marketing activity. Ask one question: *which stage of the funnel does this serve?* You will discover that 'we should use AI' is not a strategy — it's a tool reaching for an unspecified problem.
  • Step 1 — Refuse the abstract: 'Use AI for marketing' is unanswerable. Say back: 'Which funnel stage are we trying to move, and which metric will we know it by?' Don't move until you have an answer.
  • Step 2 — Pick the stage: Founder says: 'Lower CAC.' That's BOFU territory (or a positioning fix; see Step 4). Now we know what we're optimising.
  • Step 3 — Pick the metric: CAC has many cousins: blended CAC, paid CAC, CAC by channel, CAC by cohort. Pick one and write it down: 'Paid CAC for new D2C buyers acquired this month.'
  • Step 4 — Diagnose before tooling: Is the math broken (LTV/CAC < 3)? AI won't fix that. Is the creative tired? AI can help. Is the audience wrong? AI can help find a better one. Different diagnosis, different tool.
  • Step 5 — Pick the AI ratio: TOFU drafts at 80% AI, voice review human. BOFU emails 50/50, cart-recovery copy human-led. Crisis PR — human only. The ratio comes from the diagnosis, not the tool catalogue.

Move 2 — Which stage gets AI?

  • Once you can name the stage, the AI question becomes specific instead of philosophical. Here is the rule of thumb that holds across D2C, SaaS, and edtech: **as the stakes per piece rise, the human ratio rises with them.** A crisp number from a Pune D2C wellness brand scaling to Tier-2 cities last March: when they shifted ad-creative drafting from 100% human to 80% AI / 20% human review, output went 4x and CAC moved from ₹920 to ₹910 (within noise).

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: AI tactics without metrics: The single most common failure mode in 2026: someone writes 'AI ad copy' on a Q-plan, ships 200 variants, and reports 'we shipped 200 variants' as if shipping were the goal.

Takeaways (memorize these)

If LTV/CAC is under 3, you don't have a marketing engine — you have a fundraising plan, and AI cannot rescue the math.
'Use AI for marketing' is not a strategy; it's a tool reaching for an unspecified problem.
The funnel-stage lens decides everything: TOFU absorbs AI variance, BOFU pays for it in lost sales.
Diagnose before tooling: math, audience, creative, or product — each points to a different AI ratio.
If your AI plan has no 'we will move metric X to Y by date Z, or do A', you have an activity plan, not a strategy.
