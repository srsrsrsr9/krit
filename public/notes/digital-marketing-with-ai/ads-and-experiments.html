Ads, A/B Tests, and the Statistical Reality

Why your day-2 'winner' is almost always noise

notes by Meera ✏️

~ 14 min read ~

Day 2 of an A/B test. Variant B is showing a 14% lift in click-through rate.

Mental model: sample size is the unit, not days
Marketing teams measure experiments in *days* because the dashboard shows days. Statistics measures experiments in *sample size*. They are not the same.

Move 1 — The no-conclusion-yet rule

  • The no-conclusion-yet rule is two sentences: 1. Before launch, compute the minimum sample size needed to detect the *minimum lift you'd act on*, with 80% statistical power and 95% confidence.
  • Step 1 — Decide what would matter: Ask: 'What lift would change our decision?' If a 1% lift wouldn't change anything, don't run a test that needs 100k+ impressions to detect it. Pick the lift you'd act on; usually 5-10%.
  • Step 2 — Compute the sample size: For your baseline rate and minimum detectable lift, get the per-variant sample size. Free calculators do this in 30 seconds. Write the number down where the team can see it.
  • Step 3 — Estimate test duration honestly: Sample size needed / daily impressions per variant = honest minimum days. If that exceeds your patience, either pick a bigger lift to detect, or run the test on a higher-traffic asset.
  • Step 4 — Pre-register the kill criteria: Write down: 'We will not declare a winner before day X. If by day X the lift is < 3%, we'll continue running A.' This is the no-conclusion-yet rule, on paper, before launch.
  • Step 5 — On the date, look at the data and act: Not before. Not after seeing day-2 numbers. The point of pre-registration is to bind your future self.

Move 2 — Lift vs noise

  • The second move is a habit of seeing. Every time you look at a 'lift' number, ask: *what's the noise band?* If the lift is inside the noise band, the result is statistically indistinguishable from chance — even if the dashboard makes it look heroic.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: declared winner at day 2, still noise: The trap is structural, not personal. Dashboards show daily numbers; daily numbers feel meaningful; meaningful-feeling numbers get acted on. The cure is a habit, not a tool.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Sample size is the unit of an A/B test, not days — measuring in days is confusing time with information.
The no-conclusion-yet rule: until you hit the pre-registered minimum sample, the answer to 'who's winning?' is no conclusion yet.
Every 'lift' has a noise band; if the lift is inside the band, the result is indistinguishable from chance.
Pre-registration binds your future self to honest decisions before you know the result; without it, you'll find a story for whatever happened.
If your campaign volume cannot deliver an honest sample within an honest timeframe, either pick a bigger lift to detect, or skip the test entirely.
