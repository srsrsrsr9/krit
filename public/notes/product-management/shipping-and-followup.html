Shipping, Measuring, and What Comes After

Ship-day is the start of the work, not the finish line

notes by Ishaan ✏️

~ 14 min read ~

Most teams treat the launch blog as the finish line. The team writes a Slack post, the founder tweets a thread, and everyone moves on.

Mental model: ship-day is day 0, not day 30
Reframe a launch as the **start** of the data-collection phase, not the end of the work phase. The build phase ends on ship-day; the learning phase begins.

Move 1 — The pre-mortem

  • A **pre-mortem** is a 30-minute team exercise, run 1-2 weeks before launch, where you imagine the launch has failed catastrophically and you list every reason why. The frame is past-tense: *'It is now 30 days post-launch.
  • Step 1 — Schedule the meeting 1-2 weeks pre-launch: Late enough that the feature is real, early enough that you can act on findings. 30 minutes, all 4-6 people involved (PM, eng, design, CS, QA, maybe sales).
  • Step 2 — Set the past-tense frame: 'It is 30 days after launch. The feature has flopped. Tell me the story of why.' Insist on past-tense. People will slip; gently correct them.
  • Step 3 — Brainstorm silently for 5 minutes: Silent first, talking second. Otherwise the loudest voice anchors the room. Each person writes their failure modes on stickies or in a doc, no discussion.
  • Step 4 — Group, prioritize, classify: Group similar items. For each, the team votes: high / medium / low likelihood, and high / medium / low impact. Top quadrant (high likelihood, high impact) goes to 'mitigate now'. Most others go to 'accept + monitor'.
  • Step 5 — Convert mitigations to owners and dates; convert monitors to dashboard items: Every 'mitigate' item gets a name and a date before the meeting ends. Every 'monitor' item gets a metric name and a target threshold for action. Walk out with a plan, not a list of fears.

Move 2 — The 7-day post-launch loop

  • After launch, run a tight loop for the first 7 days, then a slower loop through day 30. The 7-day window is where you catch fires; the 30-day window is where you decide whether to double down, fix, or kill.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: vanity metrics on launch day: On launch day, the easiest numbers to share are the largest ones. Page views on the launch blog. Tweet impressions. Total sign-ups in the launch window. PR mentions.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Ship-day is day 0, not day 30. The product question — did this work — opens at launch and stays open for at least 30 days.
Run a pre-mortem 1-2 weeks before launch: past-tense framing ('imagine it has already failed') produces 3-4x more risks than future-tense brainstorming.
Run the 7-day post-launch loop: day 0-1 fires, day 2-7 activation, day 7-14 retention delta, day 14-30 outcome verdict. Skip rows 3 and 4 and you're shipping coin flips.
If users who tried the feature have lower retention than users who didn't, the feature is actively hurting; investigate before you spend on adoption.
On day 30, write a 3-sentence verdict and share it. The PMs who do this consistently get promoted within 18 months; the PMs who don't keep shipping features into the void.
