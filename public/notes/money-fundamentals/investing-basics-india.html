Indian retail investing is a maze designed by people who get commission for keeping you in it. Endowment policies sold as investments.

Mental model: separate insurance from investment
**Insurance is a transfer of catastrophic risk.** You pay a small fixed premium so that if a low-probability disaster hits — death, hospitalization, fire — someone else pays the big bill. The product should cost almost nothing if no disaster happens.

Move 1 — Pick passive over active (the math)

  • Active funds employ a manager who tries to beat the index. Index funds just buy the index.
  • 1. Check the expense ratio: Open the fund's factsheet. If expense ratio > 1%, the math is almost certainly against keeping it.
  • 2. Check 10-year return vs Nifty 50: Most fund-tracker apps show this. If the fund underperforms over 10 years, the manager isn't beating the market.
  • 3. Check exit load + capital gains: Switching may trigger LTCG (10% on gains > ₹1L/year). Calculate the tax cost vs the future-fee savings.
  • 4. Make the switch in chunks: Don't switch ₹50L at once if the LTCG hit is meaningful. Sell ₹10L/year (using the ₹1L LTCG exemption optimally) and redeploy to index over 5 years.
  • 5. Stop the SIP into the active fund TODAY: Even if the existing corpus stays, redirect new contributions immediately. Future-you's compounding starts now.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Separate insurance from investment — bundles like ULIPs and endowments do both jobs poorly
Pick passive (index) over active — ~80% of Indian active large-cap funds underperform after fees
Direct-plan saves 0.7-1% per year vs regular plan; one online switch, lifelong rebate
Term insurance + index fund + liquid fund + PPF/NPS = 95% of what a young Indian professional needs
'Guaranteed' is a marketing word — always calculate the IRR; below 7% means it's a debt product, not investment
