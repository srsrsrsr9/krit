The Lifestyle Inflation Trap

Why your raise feels like nothing

notes by Aanya ✏️

~ 15 min read ~

Three years ago you were making ₹6 lakh and dreaming of ₹15 lakh. Today you're at ₹15 lakh and somehow paycheck-to-paycheck.

Mental model: income vs spending vs net worth
Most people think *more income = more wealth*. It is not. **Wealth is a function of the GAP between income and spending, compounded over time.**

Move 1 — Lifestyle inflation, named and measured

  • **Lifestyle inflation** is the tendency for spending to scale with income, often invisibly. Coined informally in personal-finance writing in the 90s; now backed by behavioral economics on relative income.
  • Day 0 — pre-raise baseline: Income ₹15L. Spending ₹13L. Savings ₹2L (13%). Stable for two years.
  • Month 1 — raise lands: Income → ₹19.5L (+30%). Spending unchanged for 3 weeks. Savings rate briefly spikes to 33%.
  • Month 2 — house upgrade: Larger flat: rent ₹35K → ₹50K. Now spending ₹15K/month higher. ₹1.8L/year gone.
  • Month 4 — car upgrade: EMI ₹15K/month. ₹1.8L/year. Both upgrades together: ₹3.6L/year.
  • Year 1 — new baseline: Income ₹19.5L. Spending ₹16.6L. Savings ₹2.9L (15%). The dial moved 2 percentage points after a 30% raise. **The raise paid for the lifestyle, not the future.**

Move 2 — The 50-30-20 rule (and when to break it)

  • The 50-30-20 rule is the simplest budgeting heuristic that actually works: - **50%** to *needs* (rent, groceries, EMIs on essentials, utilities) - **30%** to *wants* (dining out, subscriptions, travel, gadgets) - **20%** to *savings + investments* (the gap that compounds) It's a starting point, not gospel. **Break the rule deliberately, not accidentally.** In high-cost cities (Mumbai, Bangalore) where rent eats 35% on its own, you might end up 60-25-15.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: lifestyle inflation accidentally: Most lifestyle inflation isn't decided. It's *defaulted to*. Your raise lands; rent comes up for renewal; the landlord proposes a 10% hike; you sign. Same for credit-card limits, EMIs, subscriptions you forgot to cancel.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Wealth = the GAP between income and spending, compounded over time — not income itself
Lifestyle inflation is the default; saving more is the deliberate act
Automate the savings move BEFORE the larger paycheck lands — adaptation works in your favor
50-30-20 is a starting point; high-cost cities skew to 60-25-15, aggressive savers to 30-20-50
Always multiply EMI by the term length — that's the real number, not the monthly cost
