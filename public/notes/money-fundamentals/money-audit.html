The 14-Day Money Audit

Three numbers, two minutes a week, one decision

Mental model: just-enough financial observability
Treat your finances like a small instrumented system. You don't need 22 metrics; you need 3 numbers, captured cheaply, looked at weekly.

Move 1 — The 3-number stack

  • Three numbers, captured every Sunday at 9 pm: 1. **Net worth** = total assets (savings + investments + property + emergency fund) − total liabilities (loans, credit-card outstanding, EMIs remaining).
  • Day 0 — Baseline: Take all 3 measurements. Net worth ₹4.2L, savings rate 12%, burn ₹85K. Don't read too hard yet — single points are noise.
  • Day 1 — Single intervention: Increase SIP by ₹10K/month. Don't change anything else. Single variable.
  • Day 7 — Mid-check: Take all 3 measurements again. Notice any drift in spending — are you compensating for the SIP by cutting something else, or by sliding into credit?
  • Day 14 — Final check: Final measurement. Net worth +₹15K (SIP impact + market drift). Savings rate 18% (up 6 points). Burn ₹84K (basically unchanged — good).
  • Day 15 — Decide: Keep, modify, or drop. Here: keep. The SIP increase didn't trigger compensatory spending. Lock in. Set a calendar reminder for the next experiment.

Move 2 — Run a 14-day single-variable experiment

  • Tracking is necessary, not sufficient. The experiment is: pick ONE intervention, run for 14 days, compare baseline to final.
  • Where you are now — Five lessons in. You walked in with one diffuse anxiety about money. You're walking out with: a model (income vs spending vs net worth), four named traps (lifestyle inflation, LIC-aunty trap, the active-fund fee drag, the dashboard delusion), two cheat codes (emergency fund + index investing), and a 14-day experimental loop with 3 numbers.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: the dashboard delusion: Saturday night. Excited. You build a 22-widget Notion dashboard with mood emoji column, automated graphs, savings-rate sparklines, currency conversion, color-coded categories, custom Hindi labels. Bahut santosh.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Three numbers — net worth, savings rate, monthly burn — are enough; more is procrastination
Savings rate is normalized to income; comparable across raises and cuts
One intervention, 14 days, before-after; that's the loop
A real effect shows in 2 of 3 numbers — favoring the favorite is the most common bug
Inconclusive is a valid outcome — run another 14 days, don't panic
