Compounding Is Not Magic, It's Patience

Small percentage changes, multiplied across decades

notes by Meera ✏️

~ 16 min read ~

Compounding is the single most important number-fact in personal finance, and almost everyone underestimates it. Not by a little.

Mental model: compounding is a hockey stick
Compound returns are a hockey stick, not a ramp. **The last decade of any 30-year run does most of the work.**

Move 1 — Time matters more than amount

  • The math: at 12% annual return (rough Indian-equity historical), money roughly doubles every 6 years. - Start with ₹1 lakh at age 25.
  • Age 25 → 60: ₹10K/month for 30 years: ₹36 lakh contributed total. Equity at 12% average. Final value: ~₹3.5 crore. Compounded gains: ~₹3.14 crore.
  • Age 35 → 60: ₹20K/month for 15 years: ₹36 lakh contributed total. Same returns. Final value: ~₹1.1 crore. Compounded gains: ~₹74 lakh.
  • The diff: Same own money. 3x outcome. The difference is purely time-in-market — the first decade did the heavy lifting.
  • The implication: When someone tells a 25-year-old 'too small to start investing right now', they are wrong by orders of magnitude. ₹2K/month at 25 beats ₹15K/month at 35 in absolute terms.
  • The fix: Start the SIP. Even ₹2K. Today. Increase as cash flow allows. The first day of the SIP is worth more than the eventual size.

Move 2 — The leakage cascade (small fees, big damage)

  • If compounding rewards small percentages over time, **leakage punishes them just as effectively**. A 1.6 percent expense ratio on an actively-managed fund vs a 0.2 percent index fund.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: 'just 1% more, sounds small': Anytime someone proposes a financial product with a fee or commission, **multiply the percentage by 30**. That's roughly the cumulative drag over a working career.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Compounding is a hockey stick — the last decade does most of the work
Time matters more than amount: 25-year-old at ₹10K/month beats 35-year-old at ₹20K/month
Rule of 72: years to double = 72 / annual return %. Memorize this number
A 1% annual fee compounds against you the same way returns compound for you — over 30 years, ~30% of your corpus
Symmetry: compounding works in reverse for credit-card debt and recurring leakage
