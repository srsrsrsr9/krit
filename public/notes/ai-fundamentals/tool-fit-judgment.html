When AI Is the Right Tool (and When It Isn't)

Tool-fit heuristics that ship products

notes by Karthik ✏️

~ 14 min read ~

Picking the right AI use case is a more important skill than picking the right model. The features that ship and the features that get rolled back differ less in their engineering than in their initial framing.

Mental model: volume × stakes × verifiability
Three axes pick whether AI fits a use case:

Move 1 — High-volume + low-stakes is the sweet spot

  • Most successful production AI features sit in the same corner: high-volume, low-stakes, high-verifiability. Why: at high volume, you can afford to build infra, evals, and monitoring.
  • 1. Volume: We get ~500 leads/day. High volume — AI infra investment is worth it.
  • 2. Stakes: Wrong category routes the lead to the wrong sales person, who routes it back. Cost: 30 min of human time. Bounded.
  • 3. Verifiability: Sales person spots a mismatched lead within seconds (it's their job). Detection is high; recovery is fast.
  • 4. Decision: SHIP. High volume + bounded stakes + high verifiability = textbook fit.
  • 5. Eval: Build a 200-lead labeled set; require >90% accuracy on a held-out 50 before launch. Add a 'low-confidence → human queue' fallback. Monitor weekly.

Move 2 — Human-in-the-loop is the pragmatic fix

  • Many use cases sit in the middle: not pure ship, not pure refuse. The fix is **human-in-the-loop (HITL)** — AI drafts, human reviews, ship the human-approved output.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Three axes pick AI fit: volume, stakes, verifiability
Sweet spot: high-volume + low-stakes + high-verifiability — start here
Never-ship zone: low-volume + high-stakes + low-verifiability — refuse regardless of model quality
HITL is the pragmatic fix for the middle: AI drafts, human approves, ship
No eval = no launch, regardless of model quality or prompt sophistication
