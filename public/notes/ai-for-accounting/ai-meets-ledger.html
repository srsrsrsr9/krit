Where AI Meets the Ledger

Patterns at scale meet patterns at scale; the math is finally on your side

notes by Aanya ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Two camps of CAs exist right now. Camp One thinks AI will replace them by 2027.

Mental model: a ledger is patterns at scale; AI is a machine for patterns at scale
Think of every accounting workflow as a pile of repeating patterns: the 12 invoices that look like the 12 from last month; the 47 vendor bills that all share a GSTIN format; the 4,200 bank lines that fall into 22 categories. **A ledger is patterns at scale. AI is a machine for patterns at scale.** The match is structural, not magical.

Move 1 — Classify-then-verify

  • Here is the first named move you will use for the rest of your career. **Classify-then-verify.** AI does the first pass — categorising, extracting, matching, with a confidence score on each row.
  • Step 1 — Standardise input: You get 4,200 invoices in 11 different formats. Five minutes of cleanup: one CSV with vendor name, GSTIN, item description, taxable value, GST amount. Don't skip this. Garbage in, confidently wrong out.
  • Step 2 — AI proposes HSN with confidence: The model returns: HSN code, 4-digit and 8-digit, plus a confidence score 0 to 100. 3,840 score above 90; 320 between 70 and 90; 40 below 70. Total runtime: 14 seconds for ₹0.42 of API spend.
  • Step 3 — CA samples the easy stuff: Pick 50 random rows from the 3,840 high-confidence pile. Verify by hand. If your sample error rate is under 1 percent, accept the pile. If it is higher, you have a systemic problem and you re-prompt.
  • Step 4 — CA reviews the medium pile: All 320 medium-confidence rows get a human eye, two seconds each, ten minutes total. You override roughly 40, accept the rest.
  • Step 5 — CA investigates the low pile: All 40 low-confidence rows get a real conversation with the file. These are typically genuine ambiguities — composite supplies, mixed packaging, exempt items mis-billed. This is exactly the work a CA is paid for.
  • Step 6 — Eval log: Every prompt, every response, every override is logged with timestamps and reviewer name. If a notice arrives in 2028, you can reproduce the entire workflow.

Move 2 — The 80/20 split between AI and CA

  • Now the bigger picture. Across the full surface of accounting work, roughly 80 percent of the hours are pattern-matching work — extraction, classification, reconciliation, drafting, summarising.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: the third-category fantasy: Every CA, on first contact with AI, tries to invent a third category — "AI does it but a human spot-checks" or "AI signs and the CA reviews afterwards." There is no third category. **Either AI does it under your supervision, or you do it. The signature is yours either way.** Anything that blurs this line is how firms get into trouble.

Takeaways (memorize these)

A ledger is patterns at scale; AI is a machine for patterns at scale; the match is structural, not magical.
Classify-then-verify: AI does the wide first pass with confidence scores; the CA does the deep second pass on flagged rows and high-rupee entries.
Roughly 80 percent of accounting hours are pattern-matching work AI eats; roughly 20 percent are judgment hours only a CA may sign.
There is no third category. Either AI does it under your supervision, or you do it; the signature is yours either way.
AI's biggest superpower is calibrated honesty — it tells you which rows it is unsure about. A junior never will.
