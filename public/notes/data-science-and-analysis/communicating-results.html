Communicating Results So They Land

BLUF, one chart, an assumption ledger; the rest is appendix

notes by Ishaan ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Most analysis dies in the inbox. Twenty-eight slides, six tabs, three regression tables, no answer in the first paragraph.

Mental model: BLUF — bottom-line up front
Hold this picture. Every memo, slide, Slack message, email opens with the answer. Not the agenda. Not the methodology. Not the caveats. The answer.

Move 1 — The BLUF chart

  • Every memo gets exactly **one chart that proves the headline**. The BLUF chart's job is to make the conclusion visually obvious in 5 seconds.
  • Step 1 — Write the headline first: Before drawing anything: write the one-sentence answer. 'Churn rose from 6.8% to 8.4% in Q3, driven by Tier B (61% of the lift).' That sentence is the chart's only job.
  • Step 2 — Pick the visual gestalt that matches the sentence: Lift over time → line chart. Comparison of two groups → side-by-side bars. Composition of a whole → stacked or treemap. Match the gestalt to the verb in the headline.
  • Step 3 — Highlight only what you mean: Reduce everything not in the headline to grey. Use one bright colour for the variable that proves the point. Five bright colours = five conclusions = no conclusion.
  • Step 4 — Annotate on-chart, not in the caption: Captions get skipped. Annotations get read. Add a short on-chart label pointing at the inflection or the gap that supports the headline.
  • Step 5 — Delete the rest: Every other chart you produced goes in the appendix. The reader who wants more will scroll. The reader who does not, you have already served.

Move 2 — The assumption-ledger appendix

  • BLUF gets you read. The **assumption-ledger appendix** gets you defended.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: ship the model, not the story: The trap that gets the most analysts: building a 92%-accurate model and assuming the *number* is the deliverable. The number is the input to the deliverable. The deliverable is a story a non-analyst can act on, with a defensible record of how the number got there.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Most analysis dies in the inbox because the artifact is wrong, not because the analysis is wrong; ship for 90-second reads, not for completeness.
BLUF is non-negotiable: sentence 1 is the answer, the recommendation, or both; methodology is appendix.
One chart per memo, and that chart must visually prove the headline in 5 seconds — highlight what you mean, annotate on-chart.
The assumption-ledger appendix is your defence when you are not in the room; without the sensitivity column, the ledger is descriptive, not defensible.
Spend the last 20% of every project on the artifact; the four weeks of analysis only matter if they land in the 90 seconds the stakeholder gives you.
