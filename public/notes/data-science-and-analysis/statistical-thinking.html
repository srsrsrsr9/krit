Statistical Thinking Without the Stats Major

Confidence intervals over point estimates; effect size over p-value

notes by Meera ✏️

~ 14 min read ~

Most of working analytics gets by on three concepts: a **confidence interval**, a **t-test**, and the back-of-envelope for **how many rows you need**. You do not need a statistics major.

Mental model: confidence intervals over point estimates
Hold this picture. Every number you produce from data is a *guess at a range*, not a single true value. The point estimate is the centre of that range. The confidence interval is the range itself. The CI tells you what you actually know.

Move 1 — The t-test that actually matters

  • You will run two tests in your career more than every other test combined: the **two-sample t-test** (comparing means of two groups) and the **two-proportion z-test** (comparing rates of two groups). Master these and 80% of business analytics is solved.
  • Step 1 — Get the business threshold first: Ask: 'What's the smallest lift that would change your decision?' If the PM says '+0.5pp conversion', that is your threshold. Without it, no test result is interpretable.
  • Step 2 — Choose the right test: Conversion rate (binary outcome): two-proportion z-test. Time-on-page or revenue per user (continuous): two-sample t-test. Pick once, before you run.
  • Step 3 — Compute the difference and the standard error: Difference of rates is straightforward; SE depends on both sample sizes and both rates. Most analytics platforms expose this; if not, scipy.stats.ttest_ind or proportions_ztest in statsmodels.
  • Step 4 — Build the 95% CI: CI ≈ difference ± 1.96 × SE. Report this in the headline. The width tells you what you can claim; the centre tells you the direction.
  • Step 5 — Compare CI to threshold: Three possible reads: entirely above (ship), entirely below (kill), or straddles (run more or accept inconclusive).
  • Step 6 — Report all three numbers, p-value last: Headline: 'Variant B converts at 5.1% vs control 4.8%; 95% CI on the difference is +0.1pp to +0.5pp; threshold was +0.5pp; recommend running for 2 more weeks.' p = 0.04 is a footnote.

Takeaways (memorize these)

'p < 0.05' is a t-shirt slogan, not a decision rule; report effect size and confidence interval first, p-value last.
Every number is a guess at a range; the confidence interval is the honest version of the point estimate.
Two tests cover 80% of business analytics: the two-proportion z-test (rates) and the two-sample t-test (means); pick the right one before running.
Compute required sample size at the start of the experiment using n ≈ 16 × p(1−p) / Δ²; if the math says impossible, do not run.
Compare your CI to a pre-stated business threshold; three reads only — ship it, kill it, or run more — and the threshold is what prevents post-hoc storytelling.
