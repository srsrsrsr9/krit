Assessment and Feedback Without Burnout

Rubrics are AI's superpower; meaning is still yours

notes by Meera ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

It is Sunday at 9 PM. Thirty Class 11 essays sit on your desk.

Mental model: rubrics are AI's superpower; meaning is yours
AI is genuinely brilliant at one thing: applying a checklist to text. Give it a 6-criterion rubric and it will tell you, in 12 seconds, which boxes a piece of writing ticks. That's not a bug; that's a feature, and it is the *only* feature you should rely on.

Move 1 — AI-drafted feedback you actually review

  • Here is the move that saves you four hours a weekend without costing your classroom anything: **AI drafts the feedback. You review every line before it goes out.** The trick is in the verbs.
  • Step 1 — Write the rubric, then write it again: Six criteria. Three or four levels each. The rubric is what AI scores against. If your rubric is fuzzy, every comment will be fuzzy. 10 minutes spent here saves 50 later.
  • Step 2 — Paste 5 essays at a time with the rubric: AI returns scored comments per essay. Don't dump all 30 at once; chunk to keep AI's context honest. 5 chunks of 6 essays.
  • Step 3 — Read the AI's comments before reading the essay: Counter-intuitive but it works. AI's comment frames your reading. You spot disagreements faster than you spot agreements.
  • Step 4 — Read the essay; flag where AI got it wrong: About 1 in 4 essays will have a meaning-level miss. AI praised an argument that doesn't hold up; AI penalised a sentence that was actually clever. These are *yours* to override.
  • Step 5 — Add one personal line per student: "Your second paragraph reminded me of the way you argued about the canteen rule last term — you're getting better at this." One line. AI cannot write this. The line is the relationship.
  • Step 6 — Send: 70 minutes total. The 12-minutes-per-student version was 6 hours. You bought back a Sunday afternoon without losing the relationship signal.

Move 2 — Formative vs summative discipline

  • Here is a small rule that solves 80% of teacher-AI grading anxiety: **AI is for formative assessment; you do summative.** Formative = work in progress. Practice essays, draft worksheets, weekly quizzes that won't appear on the final mark.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: AI feedback shipped without reading it: Sunday night. 30 essays. AI generates feedback in 4 minutes. You hit send.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Rubrics are AI's superpower; meaning is yours. Use AI for the structural pass, never for the meaning verdict.
AI drafts feedback; you review every line before it goes out. Two minutes per student replaces twelve.
The personal line per student carries the relationship. AI cannot write it. It is the difference between feedback and noise.
Formative vs summative is a hard line. AI is fine on practice work; marks-of-record stay under your hand.
If you don't have time to review AI feedback, you don't have time to use AI. The discipline is the value.
