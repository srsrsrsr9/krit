Run a 14-Day Classroom Experiment

A baseline, three numbers, and a kill criterion

notes by Ishaan ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Most of what passes for "AI works in my classroom" is vibes. A teacher tries a tool for two weeks, students seem more engaged, the teacher reports it as a win.

Mental model: a tracked intervention with three numbers
Think of the experiment as a small, tracked change. You change one thing — the AI tool — and you watch what happens to *three* numbers across two weeks.

Move 1 — Design with three numbers

  • The design fits on one page. Three numbers, three captures, one decision rule.
  • Day 0 — Capture baseline: Write down the three current values *before* starting. "Last 4 weeks: quiz avg 68%, Sunday prep 4 hrs, last student survey: 3.8/5." Five minutes.
  • Days 1-7 — Run intervention week 1: Use the AI tool consistently as designed. Don't change the design mid-week. Don't combine with other new things — that confounds the experiment.
  • Day 7 — Mid-point gut check (no decisions): Quick note on a sticky: how does it *feel*? Worth re-reading on day 14 to see how the gut compared to the data.
  • Days 8-14 — Run intervention week 2: Same tool, same design. Continue capturing the data points (weekly quiz, time logs, day-13 survey).
  • Day 14 — Capture post values: Three numbers, written down. "Quiz avg 71%, Sunday prep 2.5 hrs, survey 3.7/5." Don't editorialise — just the numbers.
  • Day 14 — Make the decision: Apply your decision rule. Outcome up, cost down, relationship roughly flat → keep. Outcome flat, cost down, relationship dropped → keep but redirect saved time. Any number breached the kill criterion → stop. The rule is the whole point.

Move 2 — Decide on day 14

  • Day 14 is the only day that matters. Everything before it is data collection; everything after is execution of the decision.

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: shipped without baseline: You started using the AI tool three weeks ago. Things 'feel better.' You're recommending it in the staff room.

Takeaways (memorize these)

An experiment is a tracked intervention with three numbers — outcome, cost, relationship — captured on day 0 and day 14.
No baseline, no experiment. The 5 minutes you spend writing baseline numbers on day 0 cannot be recovered later.
Pre-write a kill criterion. Without one, you'll protect a tool you wanted to like even when the data says stop.
The relationship signal beats outcome and cost. If 'my teacher knows me' dropped, you stop, regardless of how much time you saved.
Saved time is not the win; the win is what you redirect the saved time toward. The boring 80% buys you the human 20%.
