AI as a Teaching Assistant, Not a Replacement

The boring 80%, the human 20%, and the line you have to draw

notes by Aanya ✏️

~ 13 min read ~

Teaching is two jobs that share a body. The first job is the boring 80%: photocopying, drafting feedback stems, summarising chapters, formatting question papers, writing the same parent email for the fifteenth time.

Mental model: AI is a fluent intern who has never met your students
Picture a smart, fast, exhausting intern. Speaks in full paragraphs. Knows the textbook. Has never sat in your second-period Class 8 and watched Aarav fall asleep three times because his father lost his job last week.

Move 1 — Scope the task

  • Before you open Claude or ChatGPT, ask one question: *which 80%?* Not "how do I use AI for teaching". Not "can AI plan my whole week".
  • Step 1 — The Sunday thought: "Ugh, I have to teach the Periodic Table on Tuesday." Not yet a prompt. It's a feeling.
  • Step 2 — Identify the audience: Class 9 CBSE, mixed-ability section, 38 students. Last year's class found atomic mass confusing. That's audience-specific signal AI doesn't know.
  • Step 3 — Identify the outcome: By the end of the period, students should be able to read a periodic-table cell and tell you the element's atomic number, mass, and group. That's three concrete verbs, not a vibe.
  • Step 4 — Identify the time and constraint: 40-minute period, no lab access, board + chalk + textbook only. AI doesn't know the lab is locked on Tuesdays.
  • Step 5 — Write the prompt: "Draft a 40-minute lesson plan for Class 9 CBSE Science (mixed-ability, 38 students, no lab) so that by the end students can read a periodic-table cell and identify atomic number, atomic mass, and group. Open with a 3-minute hook. Include one quick check-for-understanding at minute 25."
  • Step 6 — Read what came back: First draft is 70% useful. You'll cut the cheesy hook, replace the example, keep the structure. 12 minutes saved on a task that used to take 35.

Move 2 — AI grades the rough, you grade the meaning

  • There is one move that, if you only learn one move from this entire course, will keep you out of trouble: **AI grades the rough, you grade the meaning.** AI is good at structure. Is the paragraph well-formed?

Traps to flag for the exam

The classic trap: letting AI grade essays unsupervised: It is so tempting. 30 essays, AI scores them all in 4 minutes, you post the marks. Don't.

Takeaways (memorize these)

Teaching is two jobs: the boring 80% (structure work AI does well) and the human 20% (judgment work AI does badly). Drawing the line is the whole skill.
AI is a fluent intern who has never met your students. Trust it accordingly.
Scope every task before opening the AI; the smaller the brief, the better the output.
AI grades the rough, you grade the meaning. Never reverse the order on marks of record.
Letting AI grade unread is the classic trap. Use it for reading order, not for the marks themselves.
